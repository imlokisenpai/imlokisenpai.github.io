Kafka wrote, “I was ashamed of myself when I realized life was a costume party.”
I never understood him.

Until the day I spoke - not as confession,
but as truth rising from somewhere deeper than words.
I only wanted to be heard.
The one I chose chose to leave.

I never told my side of the story
and in that silence, I was loved.
When truth finally gathered its storm,
I learned who stayed only for the calm.

I wore my composure well:
dependable, unshaken, intact.
Beneath it, a storm I had carried for years.
When I finally set it down,
they did not see truth
only chaos.

And chaos, people say, is best avoided.

That is when I understood Kafka.
Not because I wore a mask
but because I believed
someone might love what was underneath.
