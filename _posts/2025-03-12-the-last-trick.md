Perhaps freedom was never the absence of chains. Perhaps it was the discovery
that you had been holding them — that the difference between the prisoner and
the guard was only ever the one who thought of unlocking the door first.

I have been on both sides of a door I did not know was there.

The trick, if there is one, is this: you cannot get free by wanting to. Wanting
is a leash with better manners. You get free the way you get untied — by
noticing the knot, and then by noticing that the knot is something you did
with your own hands, for a reason you have since forgotten.

I keep waiting for the argument that ends it. Someone should walk in and say
the thing that makes the rest of this unnecessary. The thing is not making
itself known.

So I have decided, provisionally, and against my better judgement, that the
last trick is the one where you stop performing your own captivity — because a
performance, at least, can be called off.

> Whether that is freedom or simply a better habit, I do not know.
> I am leaving it unresolved on purpose.
