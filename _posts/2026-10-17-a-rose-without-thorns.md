Soon, she will be married,
and begin a family
with the man she loves.

There are things left unsaid, perhaps,
but somehow, we both know.
After all, we share the same blood.
After all, she has our mother's eyes,
filled with emotions,
yet clear enough to see through everything.

A woman born into our house,
yet so unlike the rest.
A flower without thorns,
softness without weakness.

She used to care for me
in ways no mother ever could.
Perhaps that is a sister's love,
a love that asks for nothing
yet gives you more
than you ever knew you needed.

We look so much alike,
yet stand worlds apart.

I am no good man,
just a shell filled with hatred,
jealousy,
and sins I cannot even name.

Yet she loved me.

She thought I was a genius,
and somehow,
she made me believe it too.

Perhaps she really is clever,
this beauty with a mind of her own.
How did I never realise
the way she made me feel proud of myself?

Where did all that ugliness go?
All those hateful feelings
I once carried inside me?

Perhaps they were still there,
but she never let me see myself
through their eyes.

My sister has a strange kind of grace.
She makes people better
without ever asking them to change.

She cares even when you hurt her.
She never makes her wishes
feel like burdens you must carry.

She is a flower that blooms in the mud,
yet you would never notice the earth beneath it.
You would only see the petals,
the quiet beauty,
the grace that rises above it all.

She has never demanded much.
Perhaps that is what makes her so rare.
She can calm a restless heart
like a river flowing beneath an open sky,
asking nothing of the shore
except to let it pass.

What a lucky man you are, my friend,
to have her walking beside you.

My sister,
a rose without thorns,
rain without storms,
calmness without schemes,
and strength like an ancient tree
that has weathered a thousand winters
without forgetting how to shelter others.

Soon, she will leave this house
to build one of her own.

And perhaps there will come a day
when I miss her voice in the next room,
her familiar presence,
the quiet comfort of knowing
that somewhere in this world,
my sister is near.

I have never been good
at saying what she means to me.
Some things remain buried
beneath the weight of being a brother.

But if there is one wish
I could send with her
into the life that awaits,

it would be this:

May her smile never grow weary.
May the world be gentle with her
in all the ways she has been gentle with us.

May she never have to beg for the love
she has given so freely.

And may the man who holds her hand
understand what a rare soul
he has been trusted with.

For she is my sister,
my mother's eyes
living in another face,
one of the few beautiful things
this world has given me
without asking me to deserve it.

And though she is about to become
someone else's home,

I hope she never forgets
that she will always have one
in me.
