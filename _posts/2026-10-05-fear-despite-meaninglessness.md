Death once felt kind
an end to everything,
an endless rest without thought.
Yet I fear it.
Not my own ending,
but time’s slow cruelty:
old age,
and not meeting her while I am still young,
full of borrowed fire.
I fear the erasure
not being able to remember,
or worse,
not being able to care
about the things that once meant everything.
Death frightens me
in softer forms:
the loss of those I love,
flowers- pure where I am not,
trees surrendering to winter.
I taught myself that nothing has meaning.
Still, I fear death
not as an end,
but as what it takes away:
love, beauty,
and the quiet infinities of grasslands.
