note: |-
  I have seen children from lower castes eat alone, and I sat beside them. I have seen old women forced to drink from bottles held above their lips, so their hands would never touch the vessel. And I have known what it means to have love shackled by the same chains.

  These are not distant injustices to me. They are things I have witnessed, things I have carried, and things I refuse to accept as normal.

  I did not write this out of hatred for a people, a faith, or a god. I wrote it out of contempt for every system that teaches one human being to believe they were born above another.

  No one chooses the womb they are born from. No one should have to spend their life paying for it.

They said you can love,
but only within the borders of your birth.

They said you can pray,
but only to the gods
your ancestors claimed as theirs.

They said all men are equal,
then watched an old man die of thirst
rather than let his lips touch
the water I had drunk.

They called it tradition.
I call it a crime
that survived long enough
to be mistaken for virtue.

I reject your hollow pride,
your sanctified chains,
your centuries of teaching love to kneel
before the filthy altar of caste.

I condemn your gods
if their heavens have thresholds
some feet are forbidden to cross,
if their temples have doors
that open for the powerful
and close upon the wretched.

And if your sacred water
is too pure for his hands,
then let your poison be my wine.
I would rather drink death
than beg for a place
at your table of the chosen.

Look at my veins.
The blood is red.
Not high-born red.
Not low-born red.
Not the blood of masters
or the blood of those you made to serve.

Just red.

I, too, was fed by this mother earth.
I, too, was born screaming into this world.
And I will not bow to a hierarchy
that existed before my first breath
and demands my silence until my last.

Burn me alive
for daring to name your sins.
Let the flames devour my flesh
if truth is the crime you cannot forgive.

But I will drag your holy books into the fire,
not because I fear your creator,
but because I refuse to let you hide
behind a name you have stained with blood.

Let your creator witness
what you have done in his name:
how you bleed your children
to harvest another generation of slaves,
how you teach the oppressed to worship
the very hands that keep them beneath your feet.

And how, before a child can speak,
before it can question,
before it can even know itself,
you fasten a name around its neck
and call the shackle identity.

A religion it never chose.
A sect it never questioned.
A caste it never earned.
A fate it never consented to.

You bury the child beneath its ancestors
and call the grave its heritage.

Then you dare to speak of freedom.

To hell with your inherited superiority.
To hell with the pride that needs another man beneath it.
To hell with every heaven
that demands a human being kneel to another.

I was born without your permission.
I will live without your approval.

And when the last of your borders crumbles,
when your sacred walls return to dust,
when the names you worship
are forgotten beneath the same earth
that swallows kings and beggars alike,

remember this:

You were never born above me.

You were merely taught
to stand on my neck.
