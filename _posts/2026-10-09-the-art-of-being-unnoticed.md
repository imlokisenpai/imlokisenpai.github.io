I see, I observe, and I understand.

I understand a thousand little cuts, the scars no one speaks of, and the emotions people bury beneath the ordinary business of living.

I notice.

The girl who walks alone.
The guy who lights his cigarette at the same spot, day after day.
The old women selling apple candies by the roadside.

Sometimes, even the wind,
flowing in the same direction it once did in my hometown,
carrying a fragment of somewhere I can no longer return to.

I notice these things.
Perhaps that's all I ever do.

For to them, I do not exist.
I am merely another stranger,
another passerby whose face will be forgotten before the day is over.

And yet, once, I wanted to be noticed too.

By the one who thinks of me as though I belong to some distant world, too far away to ever truly reach.
And perhaps, by the woman I loved.

I wanted someone to look at me
the way I look at the world.
To see beyond the passing figure,
to wonder what I carry inside,
to notice the little things that make me who I am.

But something in my heart has changed.

Now, I wonder if being noticed would only bring an end to this quiet dream I have built for myself.

For there is something beautiful about being a stranger to the world, about walking through its endless noise while remaining untouched by it. In those fleeting moments of solitude, I no longer feel separate from everything around me.

I feel as though I belong to it all.

The lonely girl, the man with his cigarette, the old women selling their candies, the wind that remembers my hometown.

None of them know me.
I know nothing of their lives.
And yet, for a moment, we exist together in a world that asks nothing of us.

Perhaps this is why I no longer wish to be noticed.

Because the moment someone sees me, I become someone in their eyes.
A name. A face. An expectation.
Something defined, something separate.

And I would rather remain a passing thought in a world that has never known my name, yet somehow makes room for me in every little thing.

It is strange, isn't it?

How the most meaningless moments can make you feel everything,
and how, in the very same moment, everything can mean nothing at all.
