Red.

You bleed red, as I do.

Birth arrives in red.
And death, too, wears red.

Yet you deny it.
You deny your mother's sacrifice,
the red of her labour,
the blood through which you entered this world.

You deny your own approaching death,
though it, too, may come in red.

People, people are so afraid of red.

Red stains the shackles of slaves.
Red blisters the hands of farmers.
Red is revolution.
Red is the blood of those who dare to revolt.

The sun rises in shades of red,
and even the dawn
carries its tint.

Yet you remain ignorant.
Not because you have never seen red,
but because you refuse to understand
what it demands.

Sacrifice.

The mother in labour.
The soldier at the border.
The farmer upon his land.
The revolutionary fighting for his people.

All know the price of red.

And you call yourself free.

How weak we have become,
finding comfort in our own shackles,
because imperialism has learned
to forge them in new designs.

They are softer now.
More comfortable.
Beautiful enough to wear with pride.

And they do not bleed.

You have forgotten the revolution.
You have grown content with slavery,
bound by caste, divided by religion,
enslaved by work that fattens the rich
while the poor struggle to survive.

Now you have air conditioning.
You drive through tinted windows,
watching the world pass by
without ever having to see it.

Or perhaps you see it
and have simply learned to look away.

The poor remain poor.
The uneducated remain unheard.
And you mistake your comfort
for the progress of an entire nation.

Che Guevara understood that
a revolutionary must first acquire knowledge.

But what kind of education have you received?

An education that teaches you to obey,
to compete for a place in the hierarchy,
to worship wealth,
and to mistake a better cage
for freedom itself.

The poor will continue to suffer
as long as the youth remain content
with their soft shackles.

But the day the youth understand red,
the day they understand sacrifice,
the day they recognise the chains
beneath their comfort,

that day,
the empire will begin to tremble.

And perhaps,
for the first time in generations,
red will mean more than blood.

It will mean freedom.
