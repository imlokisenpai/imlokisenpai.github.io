Sometimes, you ask me to answer the impossible,
to give a number to the beauty I see in you.

No human knows the depth of the deep sea,
and no one can count all the stars.
Yet you strike me with such sharp questions,
as though my words were made to measure the immeasurable.

Dear,
I am no god.

And even if I were,
I doubt I could measure your beauty.

For what is the worth of a god
who can create the infinite,
yet cannot find a number for you?

Perhaps that is the oldest paradox of all:
if God created a rock so heavy
that even He could not lift it,
would He still be almighty?

Then perhaps you are my paradox.

For even the gods,
who made you,
could not have known how beautiful
their creation would become.

They may have created your face,
your eyes, your smile,
but they could not have created
the measure by which they could define them.

And if the gods themselves cannot measure your beauty,
what makes you think
I could?
