Anger?
They talk like it’s an emotion.
For me, it’s architecture. It’s the structure everything else is built on.
People keep pretending I’m “naive,” like I’m too stupid to understand myself.
They don’t get it—no one does.
Because they’ve never lived in a mind that’s been burning since birth.

My son?
He knows nothing.
He hasn’t tasted the kind of rage that outlives reasons, outlives memory.
But why expect him to?
No one inherits the hell inside someone else.

Comparisons, suffering, who had it worse—what a joke.
Pain doesn’t compete.
It just exists.
Meaningless, constant, indifferent.
And everyone still pretends their agony is special.

I’m not that smiling idiot I used to be.
That smile was camouflage, not innocence.
People assumed I was harmless.
Let them.
Their assumptions die faster than I do.

The anger never left.
It’s fossilized into me, something older than thought.
Peace?
Peace is a story for people who need to feel safe.
I was never given that privilege, so I stopped believing in it.

Why don’t I say this out loud?
Because words fix nothing.
Explanation changes nothing.
People hear what they want, not what’s real.
So I write to myself.
A message thrown into a void that’s just as empty as I am.
There’s no understanding here, just observation.
Dissection.
Silence.

I remember the first time I broke—someone cried.
And instead of guilt, I felt more rage.
Not at them, but at the absurdity of the moment.
Tears, noise, fear—none of it meant anything.
I was right. Of course I was right.
But being right or wrong is irrelevant when nothing matters.

Is it my fault I see too clearly?
Is clarity a sin?
Fault is just another human invention—meaning slapped onto chaos to pretend life isn’t random.

In the end, the anger stays.
Everything else—memories, people, reasons—rots away.
What remains is the simplest truth:

I burn.
The world doesn’t care.
And neither do I.
