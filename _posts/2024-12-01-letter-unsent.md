I am not writing this to you.

I am writing it in the place where your name is, which is the only place
I have left. You are not an address any more. You are a room I keep
unlocked, and I keep walking past it, and the door is beginning to look
like a wall.

Here is what I have not said, in the order I did not say it:

That I kept the message for four days. Not the words — I could have memorised
the words. I kept the little grey square that said *delivered*, because it was
the last place your name appeared, and I wanted to see it before the morning
took it away.

That I have developed a whole personality around your absence. It is not a
good personality. It is quiet in a way people describe as *calm*.

That when it rained I would stand under the awning opposite yours and let my
coat get ruined, and it never once occurred to me to walk in.

If you read this, I will deny it. If you do not read this, then the letter
worked, which was the only thing it was for.

I am not going to sign it.

I am not going to sign it, and I am going to fold it, and I am going to put
it in the drawer that sticks, which is where I keep the things I have decided
not to be brave about.
